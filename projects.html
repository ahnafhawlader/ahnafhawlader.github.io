A Bracket That Carries Its Weight

Product design · Simulation

With a team, I designed a shelf bracket and put it through a virtual 10 kg load test. We compared steel, aluminum, and plastic, then found that a light aluminum design with a rounded corner kept stress and bending low. Tiny design details matter.

Phase Transformations in Action

Heat treatment · Hands-on experiment

I heated a metal sample and monitored its temperature in a hands-on experiment. I also studied how heat treatment changes an aluminum-copper alloy as tiny particles form inside it. Seeing processing up close made the connection between temperature, structure, and strength stick.

A closer look at materials

These projects let me play detective with structures that are too small to see by eye.

The Crystal Identity Puzzle

Materials analysis

I used X-ray diffraction patterns to identify mystery samples of iron, nickel, and cobalt, then examined the crystal structure of a nickel-cobalt alloy. It is a little like reading a material’s fingerprint, with peaks instead of prints.

XRDCrystal structures
◈

What Is Really on a Silicon Surface?

Surface analysis

A polished silicon wafer looks clean, but its surface tells another story. I used X-ray photoelectron data to find silicon, oxygen, and carbon, revealing a natural oxide layer and surface contamination. That hidden layer can change how a material performs.

XPSSurface chemistry

Textures in the Tiny World

Microscopy

I explored electron microscope images of a nickel-polymer coating, copper, and a nickel-cobalt alloy. Pores, copper grains, and fine alloy texture came into view, showing how much useful information can hide in a surface.

SEMMicrostructure

How Heat Changes an Alloy

Nanoscale analysis

I compared microscope images and diffraction patterns from nickel-cobalt samples exposed to different temperatures. The hotter sample had larger grains, a clear reminder that processing can reshape a material from the inside out.
